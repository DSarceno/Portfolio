# Content Pillar Strategy

Create **4–6 content pillars** around the topic. Pillars are repeatable themes that, in
combination, build authority, drive engagement, and support the user's offer without
sounding repetitive.

## What Each Pillar Must Include

For every pillar, produce:

- **Name** — short, memorable, topic-specific.
- **Purpose** — the strategic job this pillar does (e.g., build trust, demonstrate skill,
  start conversations, drive conversions).
- **Audience value** — what the reader gains from posts in this pillar.
- **Example post ideas** — 2–3 concrete, specific ideas (not "tips about X").
- **Best format** — the format that fits this pillar best (text, carousel, image, poll,
  article).

## A Balanced Pillar Mix

A healthy system spreads pillars across these jobs. Aim to cover most of them:

| Job | What it does | Typical format |
|-----|--------------|----------------|
| **Educate / Teach** | Concrete how-tos, frameworks, explanations | Carousel, text |
| **Authority / POV** | Opinions, predictions, myth-busting, lessons learned | Text, article |
| **Proof / Story** | Case studies, results, behind-the-scenes, personal journey | Image, text |
| **Engage / Community** | Questions, polls, hot takes that invite replies | Poll, text |
| **Convert / Offer** | Soft tie-ins to the user's product/service (≤ ~20% of posts) | Text, carousel |

## Mapping Pillars to the Goal

Bias the pillar mix toward the user's `main_goal`:

- **followers / authority** → heavier on Educate + Authority + Engage.
- **clients / sell_product** → keep Educate + Proof strong, add a deliberate Convert pillar.
- **recruiters** → Proof (results, projects) + Authority (how you think) lead.
- **educate** → Educate-dominant, with Engage to sustain reach.

## Rules

- Each pillar must be **distinct** — no two pillars should produce interchangeable posts.
- Tie at least one pillar to the `offer`, but keep overt selling to roughly one in five posts.
- Respect `avoid` — exclude any disallowed pillar themes.
- Keep names and ideas specific to the user's topic, never template-generic.
